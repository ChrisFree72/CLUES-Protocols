Sample Collection for Microplastics & Nanoplastics Analysis

Every plastic particle counted downstream has to have actually come from the sample. This guide covers what a collection kit needs, and how to keep contamination out from the moment a sample is touched to the moment it reaches the lab.

Why contamination control matters

Microplastics and nanoplastics are everywhere synthetic textiles, plastic labware, and even the ambient air have been shown to shed particles onto a sample. Unlike most analyte classes, the background level here is not zero — it's whatever a collection site and its collectors are wearing that day. A result is only meaningful if it can be shown to sit above that background, which means contamination control isn't a preamble to the protocol. It is the protocol.

The clean chain

Think of every step between the sample and the analyzer as a link. One uncontrolled link — a synthetic glove, an open bench, an unlidded tube in a breeze — can undo everything done correctly at every other step.

Keep

  • Glass, metal, and unglazed ceramic in contact with sample
  • Cotton or wool outer layers for collectors
  • Lids and covers on containers at every idle moment
  • A field blank and a procedural blank for every batch

Keep out

  • Plastic caps, tubing, funnels, or squeeze bottles
  • Synthetic fleece, polyester, or nylon near open samples
  • Aerosol sprays and perfumed products on collection day
  • Uncovered samples in moving air, indoors or out

Personal preparation

Collectors are the single largest contamination source in most microplastics studies. Preparation happens before anyone touches a sample.

  1. Wear 100% cotton or linen clothing and a cotton lab coat. Avoid fleece, polyester blends, and treated rain gear entirely on collection days.
  2. Use clean room gloves with no powder, coating, or other additives, rinsed with filtered water immediately before donning.
  3. Tie back hair and avoid synthetic hair accessories; a cotton cap or hairnet is preferred for extended sessions.
  4. Skip fragranced soap, lotion, or spray deodorant the morning of collection — these carry microplastic carriers and fibers.
  5. Keep phones, notebooks, and bags away from the immediate collection area; use a dedicated clipboard or waterproof cotton-paper log instead.

Kit materials

Anything that will touch the sample, or sit open near it, has to be glass, metal, or another inert material — never plastic. All glassware, including filtration equipment, is furnaced before use, the same as the biological sample storage protocol.

Furnaced 16 × 150mm and 20 × 150mm Kimax glass vials, foil-lined caps
Stainless steel scoops, forceps, spatulas
Furnaced glass funnels and stirring rods
Aluminum foil (for covering, never wrapping directly on wet sample)
Filtered water for rinsing tools between samples
Furnaced glass fiber filters for on-site filtration
Field logbook (cotton paper or waterproof card, not laminated plastic)
Furnaced glass only Every vial, funnel, and filter that contacts a sample must be furnaced beforehand, exactly as in the biological sample storage protocol. Furnaced glass has reduced structural integrity, so handle it gently and never assume it can take the thermal or physical stress that untreated glass can.
No plastic, no exceptions This includes plastic-coated clipboards, plastic ruler/scale bars left near open samples, and single-use plastic weigh boats. If a plastic tool is unavoidable for a measurement step, it should never contact the sample directly.

Blanks & controls

Blanks are what let a result be trusted. Every site, every day, and every batch needs its own.

Field blank A furnaced vial left open at the collection site for the same duration as active collection, then capped and processed identically to a real sample. Captures airborne and site-specific contamination.
Procedural blank Carried through every step of handling, transfer, and storage — but never actually opened at the field site. Captures contamination introduced by tools, containers, and lab handling.
Minimum frequency At least one field blank and one procedural blank per collection day.

Collection by matrix

The core clean-chain principles apply everywhere; each matrix adds its own handling detail.

Liquid (water, biological fluid)

Water & biofluids

Collect directly into a furnaced Kimax vial — never dip a plastic container first and transfer. Fill no more than ¼ of the tube volume, the same limit used for the biological storage protocol: these are furnaced tubes with reduced structural integrity, and overfilling risks cracking on top of the usual freeze-expansion concern. Cap immediately with a foil-lined lid.

If on-site filtration is required, use a furnaced glass vacuum filter holder with a furnaced glass fiber filter; stainless steel filter housings are also acceptable. Rinse the filtration setup with filtered water between samples and run a filtration blank alongside real samples.

Air / settled dust

Airborne & deposited particles

Use a furnaced glass funnel and a furnaced collection vial for passive deposition sampling; secure with foil rather than the cap during active collection to allow airflow, then cap fully once the collection period ends.

Solid (sediment, soil, tissue)

Sediment, soil & tissue

Use stainless steel scoops or furnaced forceps to transfer directly into a furnaced vial. Avoid rinsing solid samples with tap water; use filtered water only if rinsing is required. Where a sample must be homogenized in the field, use a furnaced glass or stainless steel vessel, never a plastic container.

Storage & transport

  1. Cap every vial with a foil-lined, non-plastic-contact lid immediately after collection.
  2. Keep capped vials upright in their tube rack for transport and shipment back to the lab, chilled — not inside plastic tubs or bags.
  3. Refrigerate or freeze samples according to the matrix's stability requirements as soon as possible after collection; avoid leaving samples in a warm vehicle.
  4. Store blanks alongside their matched samples at every stage — never batch all blanks together separately from the samples they're meant to represent.
Sample-type storage details For pre-cooling times, freezer temperatures, and fluid-specific handling once a vial reaches the lab, see the biological sample storage protocol aid — the furnacing, sealing, and freezing steps there apply directly to these vials.
